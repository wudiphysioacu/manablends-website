Therapeutic Benefits of Sweet Tea

Sweet tea (Lithocarpus polystachyus Rehd.) has been consumed as herbal tea to prevent and manage diabetes for a long time. Recent studies indicate that sweet tea is rich in a variety of bioactive compounds, especially a class of nonclassical flavonoids, dihydrochalcones. In order to provide a better understanding of sweet tea and its main dihydrochalcones on human health, this review mainly summarizes related literature in the recent ten years, with the potential molecular mechanisms emphatically discussed. Phlorizin, phloretin, and trilobatin, three natural sweeteners, are the main dihydrochalcones in sweet tea. In addition, sweet tea and its dihydrochalcones exhibit plenty of health benefits, such as antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, cardioprotective, hepatoprotective, antidiabetic, and anticancer effects, which are associated with the regulation of different molecular targets and signaling pathways. Therefore, sweet tea, as a rare natural source of dihydrochalcones, can be processed and developed into nutraceuticals or functional foods, with the potential application in the prevention and management of certain chronic diseases.

Therapeutic Benefits of Lavender

Emotion & Nerve System

  • Anxiety and Stress Relief

    The essential oil inside Lavender contains compounds like linalool and linalyl acetate, which interact with the nervous system to reduce stress and anxiety. Studies have shown that inhaling Lavender essential oil or using it in aromatherapy can significantly alleviate symptoms of anxiety, comparable to certain medications like benzodiazepines, but without the side effects.

  • Sleep Improvement

    Lavender is frequently used to combat insomnia and improve sleep quality. Its sedative effects are linked to its ability to increase serotonin (5-HT) levels in the brain, which helps regulate sleep. Clinical trials have demonstrated that Lavender aromatherapy can be as effective as traditional sleep aids, making it a popular natural alternative for those with sleep disorders.

  • Emotional and Cognitive Benefits

    Lavender is known to enhance mood, reduce symptoms of depression, and improve cognitive function. Its uplifting aroma can help combat fatigue and mental fog, making it a popular choice for maintaining emotional well-being.

  • Pain Relief

    Lavender has analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties, making it effective for relieving various types of pain, including headaches, menstrual cramps, and muscle soreness. It can be applied topically (diluted in a carrier oil) or inhaled to reduce discomfort and inflammation.

Immune System

  • Antimicrobial and Antifungal Properties

    Lavender essential oil is effective in combating bacterial and fungal infections. It can be used to treat minor cuts, burns, and fungal conditions like athlete's foot. Its antimicrobial properties also make it a natural option for cleaning and disinfecting.

Organ System

  • Cardiovascular and Blood Pressure Regulation

    Lavender has a relaxing effect on the cardiovascular system. It can help lower blood pressure and heart rate, making it beneficial for individuals with hypertension or stress-induced cardiovascular issues.

  • Respiratory Health

    Lavender's anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic properties make it useful for alleviating respiratory conditions such as asthma, bronchitis, and colds. Inhalation of lavender oil can help open airways and reduce symptoms like coughing and nasal congestion.

  • Digestive Support

    Lavender can aid in alleviating digestive issues such as bloating, nausea, and indigestion. Lavender tea or using it in aromatherapy can relax the digestive tract and reduce symptoms of discomfort.

  • Menstrual and Hormonal Support

    Lavender has been shown to relieve menstrual pain and reduce symptoms of premenstrual syndrome (PMS). Its calming effects also help balance mood swings and alleviate menopausal symptoms like hot flashes.

  • Skin Health

    Lavender oil is widely used in skincare due to its antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory properties. It helps treat acne, eczema, and minor wounds by preventing infections and promoting faster healing. Additionally, its antioxidant properties protect the skin from free radical damage.

The Art of the Harvest: Hand-Gathering Lavender Seeds in New Zealand

Beneath the expansive, clear skies of New Zealand, a gentle breeze rolls over rows of lavender, carrying with it a deep, earthy fragrance. For the smallholder, these fields are a labor of love. While many harvest lavender at the peak of its vibrant purple bloom for essential oils or floral arrangements, gathering lavender seeds is a distinctly different art. It is an exercise in patience, defined by the natural time gap between the plant's colorful flowering phase and the quiet maturation of its seeds.

For the artisanal farmer, securing the next generation of plants relies on a meticulous, six-step manual process that honors the rhythm of nature:

1. Awaiting the Perfect Moment (Timing the Harvest)

The harvest begins with observation. The farmer must resist the urge to cut while the flowers are in full bloom. Instead, they wait until the vibrant purple has faded, leaving the flower spikes brown, dry, and brittle. To confirm the timing, the farmer gently pries open a faded calyx (flower bud). If tiny, hard, black or dark brown granules are visible inside, the seeds have fully matured and are ready to be claimed.

2. Making the Cut

Equipped with clean, sharp pruning shears, the farmer heads into the fields. The timing of the day is just as critical as the season: the harvest must take place on a clear morning after a stretch of sunny days, but only after the morning dew has completely evaporated. This ensures the flower heads are entirely dry, preventing mold. The stems are cut long, leaving enough length to easily tie them into bundles.

3. Curing and Drying

The gathered bundles are brought out of the sun and into a well-ventilated, cool, and dry shed for secondary curing. The bundles are tied tightly and hung upside down from the rafters. To protect the precious yield, the farmer often slips a breathable paper bag over the flower heads, tying it loosely at the base. As the plants continue to dry over the coming weeks, any loose seeds naturally detach and fall safely into the bottom of the bag.

4. Threshing (Separating the Seeds)

Once the flower spikes are completely bone-dry and brittle to the touch, the extraction begins. Working over a large bowl or inside a fresh bag, the farmer uses their hands to firmly rub and crush the dried buds. The friction gently coaxes the seeds out of their husks. Alternatively, holding the stems and giving the flower heads a vigorous tap against the side of the container will send the heavy seeds tumbling down.

5. Sifting and Winnowing

The resulting mixture is a rustic blend of seeds, crushed petals, dried leaves, and stem fragments. To purify the yield, the farmer passes the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve, catching the larger debris. Then comes the delicate process of winnowing: by gently blowing across the surface of the collection, the lighter chaff and dust are carried away by the breath, leaving only the dense, dark seeds resting cleanly at the bottom of the bowl.

6. Safe Storage

Before putting them away, the farmer ensures the seeds are absolutely devoid of moisture. They are then carefully poured into airtight glass jars or breathable kraft paper bags. Sealed tightly, these containers are tucked away in a cool, dark, and dry cupboard, resting safely until the next planting season rolls around.

Precautions & Contraindications

While Lavender is generally safe, certain precautions should be taken.

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women should consult a healthcare professional before use, as lavender may affect hormonal balance.
  • Individuals with allergies to Lavender or related plants should avoid using.
  • Lavender may interact with sedatives, antidepressants, or other medications.
  • Please consult a healthcare professional if you are taking medications.

How to Brew

  1. Boil 220 mL water to 100 degrees Celsius.

  2. Put a tea bag into a cup.

  3. Pour hot water into the cup.

  4. Wait for 3 minutes.
